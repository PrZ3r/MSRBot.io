1.25-inch 3-CMOS Multi-Functional High-Speed 8K Camera System

Metadata

Publisher
SMPTE — White Plains, NY, USA
Doc Type
Journal Article
Content Type
Original Research
Abbreviated Title
SMPTE Mot. Imag. J
Volume
129, No. 6, pp. 25–34
Abstract
We develop UHD-2/8K cameras capable of high-speed recording at 120 frames/sec or more to support the highest video format of ultrahigh definition television and for shooting slow-motion video. Previously, we developed a 1.25-in. 3-CMOS UHD-2/8K 240-frames/sec high-speed camera system capable of capturing smooth slow-motion video at one-fourth speed. In this article, we describe a multi-functional high-speed 8K camera system with significantly upgraded functionality, compared with the conventional UHD-2/8K 240 frames/sec camera system. The upgraded high-speed camera has three new features. The first feature is a 480-frames/sec shooting mode. With this, we achieved twice the frame frequency of a previously developed camera system by operating the analog-to-digital converter (ADC) at twice the speed in the image sensor and subsampling the image. The video signal was processed in GBR 4:2:0 format on conventional hardware and was output in YCbCr 4:2:0 format. The second feature is a 120-frames/sec random-noise-reduction shooting mode. We applied the multisampling technique to reduce the random noise caused by the readout circuit in the image sensor. This technique can improve the signal-to-noise ratio of the camera by approximately 4 dB. The third feature is a 120-frames/sec flicker reduction mode. We generated an image with an exposure time of 1/100 sec by combining three successive frames of short- (1/600 sec), long- (1/150 sec), and short-exposure-time images to suppress the 20-Hz flicker caused by illumination with a power line frequency of 50 Hz. From the experimental results, it was found that the flicker could be reduced to 1/10 or less.

Ryohei Funatsu
bio
Ryohei Funatsu received BE and ME degrees in electrical and electronic engineering from the Tokyo Institute of Technology, Tokyo, Japan, in 2000 and 2002, respectively. He joined Japan Broadcasting Corp. (NHK), Tokyo, in 2002. Since 2004, he has been engaged in research of ultrahigh-definition TV camera systems with the NHK Science & Technology Research Laboratories (STRL), Tokyo. He is a member of the Institute of Image Information and Television Engineers of Japan (ITE).
Toshio Yasue
bio

He worked as a broadcast engineer from 2008 to 2012. Since 2012, he has been with the NHK STRL, Tokyo, where he has been engaged in research and development of image sensors and cameras for the 8K Super Hi-vision. He is a member of the Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers (IEEE) and ITE.

Conclusion We have upgraded our 8K 240-frames/sec camera system by adding three new modes: 480-frames/sec shooting, 120-frames/sec random noise reduction shooting, and 120-frames/sec flicker reduction shooting. In the 480-frames/sec shooting mode, we captured 8K 480-frames/sec images without visible lag by operating the image sensor at double the normal speed. However, the SNR of 480-frames/sec images was degraded more than expected. Therefore, it is necessary to improve image sensor operation in the 480-frames/sec mode to obtain a reasonable image quality. We also confirmed the effectiveness of random noise reduction and flicker reduction in the 120-frames/ sec shooting mode. These image quality improvement functions can be applied to various video cameras and will be introduced in future 8K cameras as required functions. We will continue to improve the image quality of the 480-frames/sec images and utilize the camera system for various 8K content productions by using the newly added functions.
