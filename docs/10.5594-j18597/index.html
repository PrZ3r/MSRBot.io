OTT (Over-The-Top) in 2015

Abstract
OTT (Over the Top) is the name given to media entertainment services delivered over the Internet. It usually refers to “premium” content services and is typically used in connection with services offered by traditional broadcast operators and content production houses over the Internet. The definition of “premium” is of course unclear and it may be taken as a purely technical catch-all e.g., high-production values, high-resolution pictures, and sound. Perhaps a better definition is “media that customers are willing to pay for” or “media for which access is managed by some kind of authentication.” This article reports on the state of the OTT sector to date.

Author(s)
Anil Kokaram
bio
Anil Kokaram is a tech lead on the Video Transcoding team at Google, where he leads a group working on video quality and develops video processing algorithms for quality improvement in various pipelines. He is also a professor at Trinity College, Dublin, Ireland, and continues to supervise a small number of students at www.sigmedia.tv in the electronic engineering department. His main expertise is in the broad areas of DSP for Video Processing, Bayesian Inference and motion estimation. He has been working in research in these areas for more than 20 years and has published more than 100 refereed papers especially in Motion Picture Restoration. In 2007, Kokaram was awarded a Science and Engineering Academy Award, with colleagues at the Foundry (thefoundry. co.uk), for his work in video processing for post-production applications. He founded a company (GreenParrotPictures) in the area of motion picture enhancement in 2004, which was acquired by Google in 2011.
Regis Crinon
bio
Regis J. Crinon is currently a technical program manager at Google Inc., where he has been responsible for the integration of the WebM VP9 Video Compression technology into the YouTube service as well as the deployment of the YouTube Quality of Experience metrics and analytics. Before Google, Crinon held engineering manager positions at Microsoft Corp. and Intel Corp. His team at Intel pioneered the development of DirectX-based filters for MPEG-2 Transport and Program Guide data. Crinon was an invited scientist at the Advanced Television Research Program at M.I.T., Cambridge, MA, where he worked with Prof. Schreiber on high-definition television. He is one of the authors of the book, “Data Broadcasting—The ATSC Data Broadcast Standard” and is the 2002 recipient of the ATSC Bernard J. Lechner Outstanding Contributor Award. He was a member of the team who received an Emmy Award for the Tektronix Profile video editing system. Crinon was also an adjunct faculty member at Oregon State University, teaching graduate-level courses in digital video compression. He is a member of the Eta Kappa Nu Honors Society.
Nicolas Catania
bio
Nicolas (Niko) Catania was engineering manager, Play Movies & TV, Google, where he led the Play Movies & TV engineering team since its inception. He helped build up the product by contributing to client software such as a ChomeCast app as well as server side infrastructure (streamers, DRM, commerce and search). He worked closely with movie studios to help define what the movies digital experience could become for users in the years to come. Before that Catania worked on the Android's media and telephony frameworks. Before joining Google Catania was a researcher in networking and multimedia at HP Labs. He held a master's in computer science from the Federal Institute of Technology of Lausanne. Catania lived in the San Francisco Bay Area with his family until his untimely passing in June 2015. Nicolas Catania passed away in June 2015. He was a well-respected and energetic engineering leader of the GooglePlay team. This article is dedicated to him.
