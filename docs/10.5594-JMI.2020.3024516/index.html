SMPTE Motion Imaging Journal ( Volume: 129, Issue: 10, 2020)
[ACTIVE]

Serving Diverse Audiences More Effective with Auxiliary Image Sequences in IMF

Metadata

Publisher
SMPTE — White Plains, NY, USA
Doc Type
Journal Article
Content Type
Original Research
Abbreviated Title
SMPTE Mot. Imag. J
Volume
129, No. 10, pp. 40–46
Abstract
This paper describes an auxiliary image sequence plug-in that enhances SMPTE ST 2067 and SMPTE TSP 2121 suite of documents to enable compositing workflows using the Interoperable Master Format (IMF). Our primary use case for the auxiliary image sequence is the management of sign-interpreted programming. This currently necessitates the creation of a new full-length master for each version created. Instead, it is proposed that the video of the sign interpreter will be stored as a separate component. This will allow the implementer to be composited with the main program when required. A working prototype of a simple compositing process has been developed by the DPP in collaboration with its member companies. Opportunities for more complex and dynamic compositing via output profile list (OPL) and time-variant metadata are also discussed in this paper, along with other use cases for the functionality described. The plug-in described in this paper benefits the implementer throughout the IMF lifecycle by enabling storage, editing, repositioning, and processing of the auxiliary image sequence before compositing.

Author(s)
Gillian Reston
bio
Gillian Reston is a lead project manager at British Broadcasting Corp. (BBC) Global News, London, U.K., where she is responsible for launching a subscription product across their digital portfolio. She has more than 15 years of experience in the media and broadcast industry and prides herself on developing and implementing efficient operational solutions for many iconic brands. Most recently, she oversaw the successful launch of Disney+ across Europe, Middle East, and Africa (EMEA), drove the integration of complex strategic initiatives, and designed best practice processes to allow cross-functional teams to work in the most efficient and cost-effective manner. During her time at the DPP, London, U.K., as the program delivery manager, she led development of a portfolio of globally recognized specifications, including SMPTE TSP-2121 Interoperable Master Format (IMF) Application DPP.
Ulrich du Bosque
bio
Ulrich Du Bosque is the test lab manager at the DPP, London, U.K., developing and running test plans for DPP-developed specifications such as AMWA AS-11 and SMPTE TSP 2121-1. He chairs the DPP output profile list (OPL) workgroup and is actively involved in developing the IMF ecosystem and spreading the word about it, including participating in SMPTE standards work and the IMF users group. He began his career as a sound recording and mastering engineer, gaining a bachelor of engineering, and then joined the BBC, London, U.K. He worked in tech support, project engineering, business analysis, and solutions design, and then moved on to the BBC Academy, London, U.K., as an engineering trainer. Subsequently, he has worked as an engineering lecturer, business change consultant, and occasionally as an electrician.
Rowan de Pomerai
bio
Rowan De Pomerai leads technology strategy and delivery for the DPP, London, U.K., the media industry’s business network. The DPP is the place where customers and suppliers come together to solve problems and create opportunities. He has an MEng degree in electronic engineering with media technology from the University of York, York, U.K. He began his career in BBC Research and Development, London, U.K., and has since delivered transformative change for major media companies including the BBC and ITV Studios. Most recently, he was the director of professional services EMEA at Ooyala, London, U.K., delivering workflow, asset management, and online video solutions to leading broadcasters, sports clubs, and enterprises.
Dan Tatut
bio
Dan Tatut benefits from more than 20 years of expertise in computer science, computer graphics, and colorimetry. He founded Chrome Imaging in 1998 and was the creator of the digital intermediate Software Matrix. In 2010, he joined Marquise Technologies, London, U.K., as VP Business Strategy and Development, developing solutions for the digital cinema post-production, the broadcast industry, and over-the-top (OTT) content providers. He has vast experience on the digital cinema package (DCP) and IMF family of standards and is also very much involved in the SMPTE 35PM group, the European Broadcasting Union (EBU) QC group, and the DPP.
Tim Davis
bio
Tim Davis is currently a principal architect at ITV, London, U.K., where he helps with innovation across the spectrum of TV production and distribution. He has 20 years of media industry background, from operations and support to technical design and delivery.
Andrew Dunne
bio
Andrew Dunne joined BBC Worldwide, London, U.K., to make CD-ROM video games, but when that market disappeared, he had to reinvent himself and moved on to video. He was responsible for getting BBC content onto mobile phones, leading to BBC channels on YouTube. He then built and ran BBC Worldwide’s internal post-production facilities, before moving on to liaising between program makers and distribution customers. This involved representing BBC Worldwide at the EBU and DPP, where he helped define SMPTE TSP-2121 IMF Application DPP. Most recently, he joined BBC Research and Development, London, U.K., to work on next-generation video systems with specific focus on ultrahigh-definition (UHD), hybrid log gamma (HLG), high dynamic range (HDR), and IMF.
