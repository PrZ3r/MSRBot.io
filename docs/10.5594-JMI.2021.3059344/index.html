Toward a Subjective Assessment System for Closed Captioning Quality

Metadata

Publisher
SMPTE — White Plains, NY, USA
Doc Type
Journal Article
Content Type
Original Research
Abbreviated Title
SMPTE Mot. Imag. J
Volume
130, No. 3, pp. 35–44
Abstract
A novel quality assessment system design for closed captioning (CC) is proposed. CC was originally designed to serve hearing-impaired audiences. Traditional quality assessment models focus on empirical methods only, measuring quantitative accuracy by counting the number of word errors in the captions for a segment. However, hearing-impaired audiences have been outspoken about their dissatisfaction with the quality of current CC. One solution to this problem may involve inviting human evaluators, who represent different groups, to assess the quality of CC at the end of each segment, but in reality, this may be difficult to do and very labor-intensive. To solve this challenge, we propose the training and use of an artificial intelligence system to predict subjective CC quality based on human assessment data. In this paper, the design and development process, to be used in developing this system, is described.

Author(s)
Somang Nam
bio
Somang Nam was born in Bielefeld, Germany, in 1989. He received an HBSc degree in computer science with a mathematics and music minor from the University of Toronto, Toronto, ON, Canada, in 2013, and the MSc degree in computer science from Ryerson University, Toronto, in 2016. He is currently a PhD candidate in industrial engineering with the University of Toronto. His main areas of research interest are human factors, human–computer interaction, and accessibility.
Deborah Fels
bio
Deborah Fels received a PhD degree in human factors from industrial engineering with the University of Toronto, Toronto, ON, Canada, in 1994. She is currently employed as a professor with the Ted Rogers School of Information Technology Management, Toronto, and the director of the Inclusive Media and Design Centre at Ryerson University, Toronto. Her research interests involve inclusive design, access to media and technology for people with disabilities and older adults, and inclusive video game design and inclusive business. She has published over 150 articles on inclusive technologies and applications and received three patents. She is also a professional engineer.
Mark Chignell
bio
Mark Chignell received a PhD degree in psychology from the University of Canterbury, Christchurch, New Zealand, and a master’s degree in industrial and systems engineering from Ohio State University, Columbus, OH. He is a professor of mechanical and industrial engineering with the University of Toronto, Toronto, ON, Canada. He carries out research in human factors and user interface design, with particular interest in health care, aging, telecommunications, and driving applications.

Conclusion In this paper, the design and development of a novel system to assess the quality of CC based on caption viewer subjective quality ratings is described. Based on findings from a user study with hearing-impaired viewers, a set of human decision probability models dealing with captioning variations was developed. With these user models and prediction models, we then generated artificial data, which reflected human assessment of caption quality. We then trained deep neural networks with the generated data, creating an AI system to predict human subjective ratings for different caption variations. The proposed solution fills a gap in existing quality assessment models for CC, which does not include any viewer quality assessment factors. The proposed system should improve as more data are collected, allowing the core prediction model to learn from the new user data over time. EXTERNAL
