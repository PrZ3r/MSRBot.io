JPEG Status and Progress Report

Abstract
The International Standardization Committee ISO/IEC JTC1 SC29 WG1, better known as the Joint Photographic Experts Group (JPEG), has a long tradition in the creation of image coding standards. More than 27 years after the release of the first JPEG standard ISO/IEC 10918, the JPEG format stands as a synonym for digital pictures.

Author(s)
Siegfried Foessel
bio
Siegfried Foessel received Diploma and PhD degrees in electrical engineering from Friedrich Alexander University, Erlangen, Germany. He is head of the Moving Picture Technologies department, at Fraunhofer IIS, Erlangen, Germany, and the head of the Technology Study Program at the University of Television and Film (HFF), Munich, Germany. His research areas include cameras, digital cinema, image compression, and post-production tools. He is a member and the chair of various national and international standardization bodies. He is a Fellow of the SMPTE and a Senior Member of the IEEE, with more than 70 publications. He is currently serving as the president of FKTG e.V., the German equivalent to SMPTE.
Jyrki Alakuijala
bio
Jyrki Alakuijala is an active member of the open source software community and a data compression researcher. He is a technical lead/manager at Google, and his recent published work has been with Zopfli, Butteraugli, Guetzli, Gipfeli, WebP lossless, Brotli and Joint Photographic Experts Group (JPEG) XL compression formats and algorithms, and two hashing algorithms, CityHash and HighwayHash. Before joining Google, he was involved in the development of software for neurosurgery and radiation therapy treatment planning.
Touradj Ebrahimi
bio
Touradj Ebrahimi is a professor at École Polytechnique Fédérale de Lausanne (EPFL), Lausanne, Switzerland, and heads its multimedia signal-processing group. He is the co-founder of the startup company RayShaper based in Crans-Montana, Switzerland, which is active in computational vision for machine- and human-centric applications. He has been the convener (chairman) of the JPEG Standardization Committee since 2014. His research interests include image and video compression, quality of experience in multimedia, and media security. He is a Fellow of the SPIE and the IEEE and has been a recipient of numerous awards and distinctions, including the IEEE Multimedia Star Innovator Award for his contributions to quality of experience assessment solutions with applications to multimedia standardization.
Pierre-Anthony Lemieux
bio
Pierre-Anthony Lemieux co-chairs the High-Throughput JPEG 2000 (HTJ2K) activity in the International Organization for Standardization/International Electrotechnical Commission (ISO/IEC) JTC 1/SC 29/WG 1 committee. He is a partner at Sandflow Consulting, San Mateo, CA, where he works with Hollywood and Silicon Valley based clients on worldwide standards and research and development of software. He has been involved in the entertainment technology ecosystem, from content authoring to consumer devices, and is an active contributor to several standards activities and open source projects. He has a PhD degree in physics from the University of California at Los Angeles (UCLA), Los Angeles, CA, and a BSc degree from McGill University, Montreal, QC, Canada, and is the SMPTE fellow and standards director.
Fernando Pereira
bio
Fernando Pereira received BS, MSc, and PhD degrees in electrical and computer engineering from the Instituto Superior Técnico (IST), Universidade de Lisboa, Lisbon, Portugal, in 1985, 1988, and 1991, respectively. He is currently with the Electrical and Computer Engineering Department, IST, and Instituto de Telecomunicações. He has authored or coauthored more than 300 papers. His areas of research interest include visual information analysis, processing, coding and description, and interactive multimedia services. He has been participating in the work of ISO/IEC Moving Picture Experts Group (MPEG) and ISO/IEC JPEG for many years, notably as the head of the Portuguese delegation, and the chair of the MPEG and JPEG requirements subgroups.
Antonio Pinheiro
bio
Antonio M. G. Pinheiro is an assistant professor at Universidade da Beira Interior (UBI), Covilha, Portugal, and a researcher at Instituto de Telecomunicações (IT), Aveiro, Portugal. He received the BSc (Licenciatura) from Instituto Superior Técnico (IST), Lisbon, Portugal, in 1988 and the PhD in electronic systems engineering from the University of Essex, Colchester, U.K., in 2002. Currently, his research interests focus on image processing, namely on multimedia quality evaluation, emerging 3D imaging technologies, and medical image analysis. He is a Portuguese delegate to ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC29, currently involved with JPEG standardization committee, the JPEG Communication Subgroup chair and participating in the JPEG Pleno standard development. He is a Senior Member of the IEEE.
Peter Schelkens
bio
Peter Schelkens holds a professorship with the Department of Electronics and Informatics, Vrije Universiteit Brussel, Brussels, Belgium, and is the university group leader at IMEC, Brussels, Belgium. In 2013, he received an EU ERC Consolidator Grant focusing on digital holography. His research interests include multidimensional signal processing encompassing the representation, communication, security, rendering, and quality assessment of these signals, while especially focusing on multidisciplinary research. He chairs the coding, test, and quality subgroup of the ISO/IEC JTC1/SC29/WG1 (JPEG) standardization committee and is an associate editor of Signal Processing: Image Communication and the Journal of Physics: Photonics.
Jon Sneyers
bio
Jon Sneyers received a PhD degree in computer science at the KU Leuven, Leuven, Belgium. He created the free lossless image format (FLIF). Since 2016, he has been a senior image researcher at Cloudinary, an SaaS technology company, Santa Clara, CA, that is a market leader in image and video management on the web.
Jan Wassenberg
bio
Jan Wassenberg is a senior software engineer at Google Research. He is the co-chair and an editor of ISO/IEC 18181, the JPEG XL standardization project. With 18 years of experience in SIMD, his work revolves around high-performance, hardware-friendly software. From 2012 to 2015, he worked on the DaVinci Resolve color correction and video editing system. In 2011, he completed a PhD degree in efficient image analysis at the Karlsruhe Institute of Technology (KIT), Karlsruhe, Germany, and Fraunhofer, Munich, Germany.
