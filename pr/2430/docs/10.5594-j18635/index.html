An Automated Tagging Approach to Improve Search and Retrieval in a Radio Archive

Metadata

Publisher
SMPTE — White Plains, NY, USA
Doc Type
Journal Article
Content Type
Original Research
Abbreviated Title
SMPTE Mot. Imag J.
Volume
124, No. 8, pp. 25–32
Abstract
Although a manual process guarantees accurate tagging of archive material, it is very time-consuming. Hence, not all media content in a big broadcast archive can be annotated sufficiently. A more automated tagging process could reduce the amount of unannotated content. This paper describes how speech technology is applied to archived content of one year of the Flemish Radio and Television Network's Radio 1 Dutch news to address the issue described. Different options are discussed and an automated approach is suggested. Techniques such as speech recognition, keyword spotting, and keyword extraction are combined to generate automatic annotations. A search engine prototype was implemented to assess the findability of the radio content. Results from the prototype show that the proposed automated approach can improve annotation and search efficiency significantly while still maintaining high precision. The results and lessons learned and presented are not only valuable to archivists, but to other professional users such as journalists and even end users.

Author(s)
M. Lycke
bio
Marieke Lycke studied electrical engineering (multimedia & signal processing) at the Katholieke Universiteit (KU) Leuven, Belgium. In 2012 she started as a researcher in VRTs Research & Innovation department where she specializes in media, mostly audio, analysis tools. She was involved in projects about employing speech and language technology in workflows for different kinds of media users: from journalist, archivist to end user. She is also a member of the EBU SCAIE group.
M. Matton
bio
Mike Matton is a senior researcher at VRT. He joined the research labs of the VRT broadcasting organization in October 2009, working on a research team on information management. His main research focuses on applying artificial intelligence techniques in the media domain, as well as methods for managing metadata, including metadata models. He has been involved in several European and national projects. Matton is chair of the expert group on automated information extraction within the EBU. He holds master's degrees in informatics and artificial intelligence and a Ph.D. in computer science from the KU Leuven. His Ph.D. research involved example-based methods for speech and pattern recognition.
L. Overmeire
bio
Luk Overmeire obtained a M.Sc. in electronics engineering at the University of Ghent, Belgium. Before joining VRT, he was a research engineer in Alcatel Bell working on software-based services and ADSL digital filter design, respectively. In 2002, he joined VRT's R&D department where he did research on metadata-based video encoding and rate control. Since 2004, he has led the competence group focusing on audiovisual content processing and file-based media production, respectively. Overmeire currently leads the research activities on enhanced end user experiences, multiplatform production workflows, and automated metadata applications. Recent research activities include innovative story creation tools based on speech technology. Overmeire is an active member of several EBU working groups. Since 1996, he was involved in patent applications and authored several papers in international conferences.
