Humans, Automation, and Workflow: Seeking Balance in Service of Art

Abstract
Since the advent of “workflow” during the Industrial Revolution, companies have been continually seeking to increase efficiency while reducing extraneous tasks or human interaction, including through automation. The media and entertainment (M&E) industry has followed a similar trajectory, accelerated most recently by the transition to digital. While automation is inherent to modern workflows, M&E relies on creativity and the subjectivity of art and therefore requires human intervention. Mitigating issues of algorithm bias, over-reliance on technology, and limited or flawed data inputs creating flawed outputs is vital in promoting healthy collaboration that allows elevated creativity.

Author(s)
Stacy Chaet
bio
Stacy Chaet has more than 12 years of experience in a variety of video post-production specialties. Currently, she is a director of workflow at Picture Shop (Formerly Sim), where she oversees media workflow teams from dailies to delivery across multiple facilities. Her teams’ mission is to implement cutting edge workflows as well as act as liaisons between different departments of production. She holds a BFA in film production from Rochester Institute of Technology (RIT), Rochester, NY, and is a graduate of the HPA’s Young Entertainment Professionals program. She has a genuine curiosity for solving technical puzzles as well as a passion for the merging of art and technology. Additionally, she is a travel enthusiast and is a mental health, diversity, and inclusion advocate.
Payton List
bio
Payton List, a LA-based Chicago native, is a general manager and workflow specialist at Picture Shop. She leads a team of incredible dailies, conform, and deliverables workflow gurus. She strives to help make clients’ lives easier, so they can focus on the creative intent of their projects, and to maintain industry relevance through R&D of new technology and software. As a leader, she values the art of effective communication, technical knowledge, diversity and inclusion, education, and collaboration. In her free time, she volunteers on the HPA Young Entertainment Professionals and Women in Post committees and enjoys speaking on industry panels.
Rachel McIntire
bio
Rachel Mcintire is a workflow producer with more than ten years of experience in dailies, VFX, and post workflow. Currently, working at Picture Shop (formerly Sim), McIntire has traveled around the world, setting up remote dailies and training new technicians. Graduating from Ithaca College, Ithaca, NY, in 2007, and working in post ever since, McIntire is also an award-winning editor, avid outdoor person, and believes strongly in promoting diversity, equity, and inclusion in post. McIntire is agender and uses no pronouns.

Blacks in STEM Jobs Are Especially Concerned About Diversity and Discrimination Conclusion There is no question about the direction the industry is heading when it comes to incorporating AI and machine learning. As this technology improves, newer algorithms are being written and neural networks are being created to “think” more like a human brain. However, it is still a complicated process that relies on humans training the systems to search for specific data sets and modeling of human behavior. And ultimately, M&E is oriented around audiovisual art, which will always have a high level of subjectivity. We will best integrate art with automation by embracing our humanity further. We need to recognize how our human needs, curiosities, and perspectives make workflows better. We must prioritize encouraging people from diverse backgrounds to become workflow professionals so that their unique perspectives can contribute to pushing the M&E industry to be more efficient for all types of people and content. As we continue to learn about and shape these workflows, we need to engage more with experts in AI ethics and data science to further understand possibilities and pitfalls. Until technology can fully replicate human behavior and psychology, humans will always be a necessary part of a workflow. Making sure we address the shortfalls of technology while establishing the best environment for humans to succeed is important. We must also remember the workflow of the past to not fall into the same ethical traps as our predecessors. By having a diverse talent pool, working to combat the biases inherent in AI, and using automation intentionally and thoughtfully, we can create inclusive and efficient workflows to support an industry of authentic storytelling.
